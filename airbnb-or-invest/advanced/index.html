How this works. Both paths start with the same money. On the Airbnb path it buys the place (or, if you own it, the sale you pass up), or buys the plot and draws the build cost evenly over the construction months while the rest keeps earning. Then it pays for the furnishing, fills up gradually over the months it takes to earn reviews, and collects bookings net of the platform's cut, management, cleaning per stay, bills, repairs, wear on the furnishings and tax. Every shilling it makes goes into the same investment the other path holds. Net worth at year 10 is the property's value less selling costs and capital gains tax, plus that pot. The market path compounds at your return, net of tax and fees.

If you own the place and would keep it anyway, the house is the same on both paths and cancels out — its value, growth, fixed costs and sale are left out, and only the furnishing is weighed against the market.

Not modelled: mortgages or construction loans — this compares cash against cash. Nor are seasonality (occupancy is a year-round average), minimum-stay rules, licensing and tourism levies, building rules that ban short lets, or the value of your own time if you run it yourself. Furnishings are worth nothing at the end. Returns and growth rates are steady averages here; real ones arrive in lumps.

This is a model, not advice. It does what you tell it to. The numbers you put in matter far more than the arithmetic it does with them.
