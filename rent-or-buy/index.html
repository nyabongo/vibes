How this works. Both paths start with the same cash. The renter invests the deposit and purchase costs on day one, and whichever path has the cheaper month invests the difference. Net worth at year 10 is the sale price minus selling costs, remaining loan and capital gains tax, plus whatever's in the investment pot. When the property is a let, your own rent is the same either way, so it's left out entirely — the comparison is the rental property against the same money in the market.

Not modelled: mortgage insurance, service charge arrears, ground rent, the cost of moving, or the fact that a landlord can raise your rent and a bank can't raise your fixed repayment. Appreciation and returns are steady averages here; real ones arrive in lumps.
